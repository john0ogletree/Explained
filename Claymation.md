# Introduction to Claymation

**Claymation** (short for *clay animation*) is a classic form of stop-motion animation where figures, props, and environments are constructed out of a malleable substance—traditionally plasticine clay—and manipulated frame-by-frame to simulate fluid motion.

---

## 1. Key Characteristics

* **Stop-Motion Technique:** Captured one frame at a time. Animators slightly move or reshape clay models between shots.
* **Tactile Aesthetics:** Visible fingerprints, surface textures, and organic imperfections give claymation a distinct human feel.
* **Metamorphosis & Squall:** Clay allows objects to dynamically melt, stretch, morph, or transform into entirely different shapes seamlessly.
* **Armatures:** Rigid internal skeletons (usually made of aluminum wire or ball-and-socket joints) support clay figures and help them hold poses without collapsing under their own weight.

---

## 2. The Production Workflow

```text
[ Concept & Character Design ]
             │
             ▼
[ Armature Fabrication & Sculpting ]
             │
             ▼
[ Set Building & Lighting ]
             │
             ▼
[ Frame-by-Frame Shooting ] (12 or 24 frames/sec)
             │
             ▼
[ Post-Production & Sound Design ]
```

### Key Stages
1. **Sculpting & Casting:** Characters are sculpted using non-drying modeling clay (like Plastilina) over internal wire armatures.
2. **Set Construction:** Miniature sets are built with rigid materials to ensure nothing shifts accidentally between frames.
3. **Rigging & Capture:** Specialized stop-motion software (e.g., Dragonframe) controls digital cameras and onion-skinning to preview movements frame-by-frame.
4. **Post-Processing:** Rigs, wires, and support stands holding flying or jumping puppets are digitally erased.

---

## 3. Core Terminology & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Onion Skinning** | Overlaying the current camera view on previous frames to gauge movement increments. |
| **Shooting on Twos** | Capturing 12 distinct poses per second (each exposed for 2 frames) to achieve 24fps output. |
| **Replacement Animation** | Using pre-molded swappable face parts (mouths/eyes) rather than re-sculpting expressions manually. |
| **Plasticine** | Non-hardening oil-based clay ideal for long shoots under warm production lights. |

---

## 4. Notable Pioneers & Works

* **Will Vinton:** Coined the trademarked term "Claymation" in the 1970s and created famous works like *The California Raisins*.
* **Aardman Animations:** The premier UK studio responsible for *Wallace & Gromit*, *Chicken Run*, and *Shaun the Sheep*.
* **Art Clokey:** Created *Gumby* and *Pokey* in the 1950s, popularizing clay animation on television.
* **Nick Park:** Academy Award-winning director behind Aardman’s most iconic clay characters.
