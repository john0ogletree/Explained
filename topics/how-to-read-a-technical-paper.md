# How to Read a Technical Paper

Most people read papers wrong. They start at the top, read every word in order, and give up around page 3 when the math gets dense. Then they decide the paper was "too hard" and move on.

The paper wasn't too hard. The approach was.

## The Three-Pass Method

Read the paper three times, each pass with a different goal.

- **First pass — the shape.**
  - Read the title, abstract, intro, and section headings.
  - Look at the figures and their captions.
  - Skim the conclusion.
  - Goal: know what the paper claims and whether it's worth a second pass.
  - Time: 5–10 minutes.

- **Second pass — the argument.**
  - Read everything except the proofs and derivations.
  - Mark unfamiliar terms, don't chase them yet.
  - Trace the logic from claim to evidence.
  - Goal: understand *what* the authors did and *why* they think it works.
  - Time: 30–60 minutes.

- **Third pass — the details.**
  - Read the proofs, the experiments, the appendices.
  - Re-derive key steps on paper.
  - Ask: what would break this? What did they not test?
  - Goal: be able to critique or reproduce it.
  - Time: hours, days, or never — depends on why you're reading.

## What To Skip on the First Pass

- Every equation. You don't need them to understand the claim.
- Related work. Come back once you care.
- Implementation details. They're for the second pass.
- Footnotes. Almost always optional.
- The references section. You're not writing a survey.

## Signals a Paper Is Worth the Second Pass

- The abstract makes a **specific, falsifiable claim**.
- Figures have **error bars** and labeled axes.
- The method section **explains choices**, not just states them.
- Limitations are **named**, not buried.
- The conclusion **doesn't overclaim**.

## Signals To Stop Reading

- The abstract is vague or buzzword-heavy.
- Figures have no error bars or axes labels.
- The method is described in a single paragraph.
- There's no limitations section.
- Every result is "state of the art."
- The conclusion claims things the experiments didn't test.

## The Thing Nobody Tells You

You are allowed to read a paper for one specific thing and ignore the rest. You don't owe the authors a full read. You're not being graded.

If you only want the method — read the method. If you only want the dataset — skip to the appendix. If you only want the citation for something you already know — read the abstract and stop.

> The goal isn't to finish papers. The goal is to extract what you need and move on.
